## Design Context

### Users

Exposure Academy serves high-school students learning and competing in practical AI/software
tracks, plus instructors who form teams, review submissions, and operate tournaments. Students
need to understand their next action, see trustworthy live progress, and compare results without
having to understand the underlying infrastructure. Instructors need dense operational status and
safe recovery controls at a glance.

### Brand Personality

Confident, rigorous, energetic. The experience should feel like the existing Exposure member
portal: a serious technical academy with visible momentum, not a game-themed microsite or a
generic enterprise dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction

Match the established authenticated portal exactly. Use the black canvas, zinc sidebar and panel
scale, Geist typography, compact uppercase eyebrows, restrained 12–16 px radii, and the single
Exposure blue accent (`#0339A6`) through existing tokens. Preserve the current light cream public
and auth surfaces. Tournament boards may use functional Monopoly property/player colors inside
the board itself, while surrounding controls and data remain in the portal system. Support the
existing responsive breakpoints and avoid decorative gradients, glow-heavy gaming UI, glass
cards, or a separate visual language for AI features.

### Design Principles

1. Reuse existing portal primitives—panels, chips, status pills, tables, buttons, typography, and
   spacing—before adding a feature-specific class.
2. Make state unmistakable: queued, live, complete, partial, failed, and disqualified must combine
   plain-language labels with shape or text, never color alone.
3. Keep competition data auditable: expose seats, strikes, final worth, timing ranges, duration,
   worker readiness, and replay links without decorative metrics.
4. Use progressive density: the submission page stays approachable; live boards and instructor
   views can be information-rich because their users are actively monitoring a match.
5. Preserve accessibility basics: semantic links/forms/tables, visible focus behavior, escaped
   untrusted text, readable contrast, responsive layouts, and no essential hover-only controls.
